Section 1: Preparation and the First Days

What is addiction?

Understand the biological, psychological, and social sides of addiction through nicotine, the reward system, and learned behaviors.

2-minute read Article 1 of 9 in this section

In this article

  1. Nicotine and the reward system
  2. What changes over time?
  3. The three sides of addiction

Smoking addiction isn't just a matter of habit or willpower. Nicotine's effects on the brain, daily routines, and social surroundings can gradually build a cycle that feeds itself.

Nicotine and the reward system

Nicotine reaches the brain quickly and affects systems tied to reward, motivation, and learning. The shift in dopamine release helps the brain learn to treat smoking as a behavior worth repeating.

What changes over time?

As regular use continues, the brain adapts to nicotine. When nicotine levels drop, withdrawal signs like restlessness, difficulty concentrating, or craving can appear. Because smoking briefly eases this discomfort, the cycle grows stronger again.

The three sides of addiction

The biological side relates to nicotine and withdrawal, the psychological side to the sense of relief or reward attached to smoking, and the social-behavioral side to pairings with coffee, meals, breaks, friend groups, or alcohol.

Key takeaways

Addiction doesn't come from a single cause. Nicotine, thoughts, and daily habits work together — noticing all three matters while you quit.
