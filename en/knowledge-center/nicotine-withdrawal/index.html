Section 1: Preparation and the First Days

Withdrawal

Understand why withdrawal happens, what kinds of symptoms it can involve, and that it's part of a temporary adjustment process.

2-minute read Article 8 of 9 in this section

In this article

  1. What might you feel?
  2. What does it mean?
  3. Approach

When nicotine decreases, the brain and body try to adjust to the new situation for a while. Some temporary symptoms can appear during this period.

What might you feel?

Restlessness, difficulty concentrating, changes in appetite, cravings, and similar symptoms can appear in the early period of quitting.

What does it mean?

These symptoms appearing doesn't mean "you need a cigarette." It can simply be a period where your body is adjusting to a nicotine-free routine.

Approach

Sleep, regular water intake, short walks, breathing exercises, reducing triggers, and seeking professional support when needed can make this period more manageable.

Key takeaways

Withdrawal can be uncomfortable, but it's a temporary adjustment process. If symptoms become too intense or unmanageable, get support from a health professional.
